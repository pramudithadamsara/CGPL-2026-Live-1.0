The rhythmic sound of the 'Peththa' knife against the moist cinnamon wood is a heartbeat that has echoed through the valleys of southern Sri Lanka for generations. Unlike any other spice, the harvest of Ceylon Cinnamon—real cinnamon—is not a mechanical feat, but a highly specialized artisanal ritual. It is a process that demands patience, precision, and an intimate understanding of the tree’s biological response to the seasons. At Ceylonspizee Global, we view this not as a mere production line, but as a preservation of a century-old heritage.

The Peeling Process

Harvesting occurs twice yearly, following the monsoon rains when the rising sap makes the bark most pliable. The 'peeler' must first select a branch of appropriate maturity—typically two years of growth. After removing the leaves and small twigs, the outer rough bark is gently scraped away. Then comes the master stroke: the rubbing of the branch with a brass rod to loosen the inner bark from the hardwood.

Quality Grading

Once the bark is peeled in long, translucent strips, it is inserted into one another to form a 'quill'. These quills are then air-dried in the shade, where they naturally curl into the familiar cigar-like shapes. The grading—from the ultra-fine 'Alba' to the robust 'C5 Special'—is determined by the diameter of the quill and the number of layers it contains.
